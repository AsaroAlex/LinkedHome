# Independent economic challenge — LinkedHome/Soglia

Date: 3 October 2026. Review of the proposed paid assisted vacancy dossier, based on the user's attached request and current official-source extracts. No contacts, purchases, campaigns, interviews or sales have been carried out. Every experimental threshold below is provisional and directional, not statistical proof.

## Recommendation changes that matter

1. **Sell delegation of an existing administrative task, not a premium income report.** FidoAffitti's current terms quote Standard €29 + IVA and Premium €39 + IVA; current homepage also quotes those numbers. Terms expressly say open banking is not currently available, so it is not a fully equivalent bank-income check. Current agency packs advertised in the provider workstream lower the approximate benchmark to €14.90 per check (Business €149/~10) and about €11.86 (Enterprise €249/~21), with credit consumption and IVA still to confirm; these are not Soglia wholesale costs. CRIF AFFITTABILE quotes €39 retail, but this is tenant-activated and requires online banking; its activation identity/terms differ from a landlord-paid API integration. Nevertheless, both anchor the report-only price. A €99 net assisted dossier must demonstrably remove work that the agency still performs with its existing CRM and existing report provider. It cannot assume the customer's entire manual process remains available to save.

2. **Use workload/value arithmetic to falsify €99.** At agency labour value €35/hour, €99 requires 2.83 hours of saved work if time is the only benefit and no verification is currently purchased. If the agency already buys two €29 reports, the incremental premium is €41 and requires 1.17 hours, provided Soglia supplies the same report scope on the same conditions. This last proviso is not currently established. Against two Business-pack checks at approximately €14.90 each, the premium is €69.20 and requires 1.98 hours at €35/hour: use the actual paid baseline, not the most expensive public retail benchmark. Buyer time value, report purchase frequency and prior spend need receipts or observed workflow; asking if the service sounds useful supplies none of them. Employee minutes removed are capacity value, not automatically cash savings. Distinguish true avoidable cost, usable capacity and an unproven higher close rate.

3. **Qualify by observed throughput and painful workflow, not simply agency size.** At least 40 residential leases/year is a reasonable screening hypothesis, but is not evidence of 40 purchases/year. At that volume, 25% adoption gives only 10 paid dossiers/year before consent failures or repeat checks. More efficient specialist agencies may already have a strong document process and lower willingness to pay. Buyer pays, employee uses, landlord receives decision support, tenant voluntarily supplies data: these distinct roles create adoption friction even when the economic buyer agrees.

4. **Fix the commercial unit before forecasting.** Define one billable dossier as one identified agency client's vacancy and one bounded execution window, with up to two initially nominated candidates, explicit included labour/support ceiling, delivery based on documents actually supplied and lawful sharing, and no promise that a tenant qualifies or the vacancy is filled. A third candidate, changed household, guarantor, second bank connection, recheck or reopened vacancy is a distinct cost event. 'Practice', 'candidate report', 'vacancy' and 'lease signed' are not interchangeable. Include non-completers in cost calculations rather than only successful delivered reports.

5. **Do not launch an income-verification promise on an unquoted provider.** Tink's Income Check is enterprise-only with personalised new-customer pricing. The specific Income Check SDK documentation does list Italy (IT), strengthening technical plausibility, but product-country support is not a signed affordable Italian income-classifier contract or validated accuracy for Italian user groups. Public report retail prices do not establish B2B sublicensing rights, automated access or agency/tenant sharing. If a partner is unavailable, the valid pilot is document collection, completeness and factual reconciliation, clearly described as such; it has less claim to the willingness to pay for a verified income statement. Test that lean offer independently.

6. **E is a different operational product.** €79/month + €39/dossier costs €2,508/year at 40 dossiers, versus €3,960 for D at €99. At eight dossiers/month E is effectively €48.88/dossier. This can only preserve margins if included operations, candidate limits and minutes differ materially. Present E as self-service workflow plus one limited check, with assisted delegation charged separately; do not describe it as the same D service under a recurring invoice. At the central rate of one paid dossier/month, E costs €118/month versus D €99, a 19.2% higher buyer charge for less assistance. The preferable E financial result therefore also assumes higher willingness to pay, not merely lower operational cost. An upgrade is commercially clearer at ≥2 paid dossiers/month (effective €78.50 each), or only if the independent workflow subscription value is measured. An agency voluntarily adopts E only after demonstrated recurrence and baseline replacement value; subscription collection does not create real recurrence.

7. **Distribution beats an unsupported city winner.** Bologna can be a research basin; it is not an approved launch geography. A remote B2B pilot avoids most city-specific marketplace liquidity costs. Choice between Bologna, Padova/Verona or another city should depend on a count of accessible eligible paying agencies and founder cost of reaching them, rather than on rents or housing pressure. City-level new-contract statistics identify activity, not agency-addressable paid dossiers or unique clients.

8. **Protect voluntary sharing without selling an unusable paid outcome.** Fido's terms explicitly permit the tenant to withhold the completed report, in which case the landlord sees only completion status; the price is not refunded for a report produced and then withheld. This is evidence of a competitor's policy, not a legal requirement that Soglia copy it. Soglia must quote the paid administrative deliverables and refund/credit rules before purchase, meter costs from all attempted candidates and separate generation, tenant approval and buyer access rates. Prechecking bank support can prevent some failures; it cannot eliminate nonconsent and discretionary nonsharing. A refusal should not create a risk score or de facto adverse inference.

9. **Legal classification follows operations.** No score, no guarantee and a fixed price reduce some scope, but do not independently establish exemption from property brokerage, GDPR obligations or financial-service perimeter. Bring-your-own-candidates, no negotiation/introduction/matching and administrative factual output are a stronger narrow pilot definition. Provider consent and tenant choice do not by themselves settle Soglia's own controller/processor role, security, retention, onward disclosure or automated-decision responsibilities. Budget a concrete flow-specific legal review before processing real income/bank records, rather than a generic disclaimer.

## Provisional falsification thresholds

- **Commercial:** interview 10–12 genuinely eligible agencies using their last completed lease, current spend and workflow; obtain at least three unrelated paying agencies at the quoted scope/price. Three payments permit a larger pilot, not a claim of validated demand. A payment/refundable deposit must be distinguished from a letter of interest. Explore €79/€129 offers without treating a tiny sample as an A/B experiment; keep primary €99 scope comparable.
- **Value:** deliver at least eight real bounded dossiers; measure employee time before/after against the same existing CRM and report alternative. Require buyer-valued recoverable capacity plus actual avoided spend to cover the quoted price for a clear majority of pilot dossiers. The hourly value is a measured buyer assumption. Unmeasured reduced default risk should contribute zero to this pilot valuation.
- **Delivery economics:** evaluate contribution after all retries, noncompleters, refunds, manual follow-up and activation effort, and after valuing operating founder time. A practical advance gate is a recurring contribution of at least €40 per €99 dossier after operating labour, with the operative time ceiling solved from actual provider cost and chosen margin. Under the model's central €30/hour founder value and provider assumptions, recurring delivery is about 64 minutes and leaves €42.53 before acquisition; about 69 minutes is the mathematical €40 ceiling. First-client activation costs and acquisition must then be recovered within three paid dossiers; do not require a margin gate that silently excludes renter activation, support or disputes. If the provider is €29 per candidate and two are used, even zero labour leaves only about €39 after payment fees, so this gate cannot be met. Renegotiate provider, limit included check count or change offer/price; do not treat this as a small sensitivity.
- **Repeat:** require at least two agencies to buy again at full price when they actually have another eligible vacancy, and observe acquisition cost recovery within no more than three paid dossiers. If no new vacancy occurs during 30 days, repeat demand remains unobserved; it is not subscription churn. Extend only this observation window rather than assuming repeat.
- **Consent:** record candidates invited, consented, generated and voluntarily shared as separate denominators; a provisional ≥80% buyer-accessible usable output gate is sensible only for invited eligible candidates and must be read alongside costs of the other 20%. Below the gate, modify intake and deliverable or stop the optional verification component. Do not coerce sharing to hit the rate.
- **Investment:** do not proceed to marketplace growth or a broad API build on these small pilot results. Continue only if willingness to pay, observed time value, variable economics and repeat need all coexist. A favourable margin assumption alone is insufficient.

At 5% monthly commercial-relationship loss, 12-month cohort retention is **54.036%**; frequency is conditional on the relationship surviving. Recurring B/D relationship loss is not observed SaaS cancellation. At stationary central D economic break-even, 45.8203 active clients imply approximately 549.8435 dossiers/year and €50,161.40 recognised annual revenue. This threshold replaces churn with 2.291 paying clients/month; month 24's growing cohort acquires five, so its result remains **−€211.70** despite 49.15 active clients exceeding the stationary threshold. No contradiction: steady-state costs differ from growth costs.

Setup founder hours are now spread over the first three operating months and the 160-hour monthly founder cap includes them; excess variable work becomes paid contractor work. No hour is counted as both founder labour and contractor cash. Founder return correctly uses cash after setup divided by actual founder hours, avoiding a second deduction of his hourly opportunity cost.

The central commercial ramp requires 73.5 newly paying buyers in 24 months. At the independent hypothetical qualification/conversion funnel of 40% × 30% = 12%, this implies roughly **612.5 researched operator names**, exceeding the proposed initial 250-name list. This is an unverified sourcing requirement, not proof those operators can be obtained. D favourable is a performance envelope conditional on many more paying buyers and contractor hours, not a demand forecast.

The audit supports **D as the cheaper first experiment**, not as a demonstrated highest-profit configuration. E's central 24-month operating/project results are less negative and its final month is profitable under its distinct self-service/one-check scope, but neither central scenario has recovered total economic costs. Keep this distinction in the opening recommendation. D is a sensible vehicle to observe administrative value and payer recurrence before investing in E; the economic evidence does not justify an immediate full operating company or marketplace launch.
